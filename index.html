Instagram Group Chat Guide

How to set up a group message to coordinate post resharing with your branches, step by step with screenshots.

What This Solves

Right now, asking branches to reshare AMWA posts means sending mass emails every time. An Instagram group chat gives you a faster, simpler way to share a post and ask everyone to reshare it, all in one message, right where the content lives.

How it works in practice

You publish a post on the AMWA Instagram account, share it into the group chat, and add a quick note like "Please reshare to your Stories this week." Branches tap the post right in the chat and reshare it with one tap.

Step 1: Open Your Messages

Tap the message icon in the top right corner of Instagram. It looks like a paper airplane (or the Messenger icon on some versions).

From your Instagram home feed or profile, look at the top right corner. You will see a paper airplane icon (or a chat bubble with a lightning bolt on newer versions). Tap it to open your Direct Messages inbox.

If you are on the AMWA business account, make sure you are logged into that account before starting, not your personal account.

Step 2: Start a New Group Message

Tap the compose icon (pencil) in the top right, then search for and select each branch account.

Once your inbox opens, tap the pencil/compose icon in the top right. This opens the "New Message" screen.

In the "To:" field at the top, search for each branch's Instagram handle one at a time. Tap each one to add them to the group. You will see blue chips appear as you add people.

Once you have added everyone, tap "Chat" at the top right to create the group.

Step 4: Share a Post to the Group

When you publish a new post, share it directly into the group chat so branches can reshare it.

  1. Go to the post on the AMWA profile
  2. Tap the share icon (paper airplane) underneath the post
  3. A share sheet appears - select the group chat from the list
  4. Add a quick note like "Please reshare to your Stories by Friday!"
  5. Hit Send

The post will appear in the chat as a tappable card. Branches can tap it, then tap "Add to Story" to reshare with one tap.

Step 5: What Branches See

Here is what it looks like from a branch's perspective when they open the group chat.

Branches open the group chat and see the shared post as a tappable card along with your message.

To reshare, they tap the post card, which opens it full-screen. From there they can:

  • Add to Story - one tap, disappears after 24 hours (easiest ask)
  • Repost to Feed - creates a permanent post on their profile (bigger ask)
Best practice

Default to asking for Story reshares. They are quick and low-effort for branches. Save feed reposts for your most important content.

Limits and Restrictions

Everything you need to know about what Instagram does and does not allow with group chats.

Limit Details
Group size Up to 250 members per group (including you). If AMWA has more than 249 branches, you would create a second group.
Daily messages Instagram allows roughly 50 to 200 messages per day depending on account age and history. For a few reshare requests per week, you will never come close to this.
Message length 1,000 characters per message (including spaces). More than enough for a reshare request.
Forwarding You can only forward a message to 5 chats at a time. Not an issue since you are posting directly into one group.
Who can be added Branch accounts must follow the AMWA account or have DM requests open. Otherwise your messages go to their "Requests" folder and may be missed.
Business account label When a Business account sends a DM, Instagram shows a small gray "business chat" label. This is normal and cosmetic only.
Before you create the group

Send a quick email to branches asking them to follow the main AMWA Instagram account first. This ensures they will receive the group messages in their primary inbox, not buried in Message Requests.

What about spam flags?

Instagram watches for spammy behavior (mass messaging, rapid-fire sending, repetitive text). Sending a few reshare requests per week to a group of people who already follow you is completely normal use. You will not trigger any restrictions. If you ever did get temporarily limited, it lifts automatically in 24 to 48 hours.

Tips for Keeping the Group Effective

📌

Pin important messages

Long-press a message to pin it to the top. Great for standing instructions like "reshare within 48 hours."

🔇

Muting is okay

Let branches know they can mute notifications. They will still see messages when they open the chat.

🎯

Keep it focused

Use this group only for reshare requests. If it turns into a general chat, people will stop checking it.

📅

Set clear deadlines

"Please reshare by Friday" gets much better follow-through than "please share this."

Alternative: Broadcast Channel

Instagram also offers Broadcast Channels. Here is how they compare so you can decide what works best.

Recommended

Group Chat

Everyone can send and read messages. Works like a group text.

  • Two-way communication
  • Branches can confirm they reshared
  • Can ask questions or coordinate
  • Simple to set up
  • Max 250 members
  • Can get noisy if people chat

Broadcast Channel

Only you can send messages. Branches can react with emojis and polls only.

  • No member limit
  • Stays clean and organized
  • Feels more official
  • Branches cannot reply
  • No confirmation of reshares
  • Less personal
Our recommendation

Start with the group chat. It gives you two-way communication, which is valuable when building this habit with branches. If the group gets too large (250+) or too noisy, you can always switch to a Broadcast Channel later.

Quick Start Checklist

  1. Email branches first - ask everyone to follow the main AMWA Instagram account (so they receive the group messages)
  2. Create the group chat - follow Steps 1 through 3 above
  3. Send a welcome message - introduce the purpose: "This group is for reshare requests. When we post something, we will drop it here and ask you to share it to your Stories."
  4. Share your first post - follow Steps 4 and 5 above to test the flow
